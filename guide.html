Digital Surveillance Resource Guide

This guide introduces digital surveillance technology and explores how it affects privacy, law enforcement, and public safety. It is designed for people who are new to the topic.

Why this issue matters

Digital surveillance is rapidly becoming a bigger part of everyday life. Things like security cameras, facial recognition, and license plate readers make it possible for law enforcement to collect information about people.

These technologies may improve public safety and assist with police investigations, but they raise a concern about privacy.

It is important for people to understand both sides of digital surveillance. Technology can be useful, but there should also be limits on how personal information is collected and used.

One helpful resource is the Atlas of Surveillance. It shows which surveillance technologies law enforcement agencies use across the United States.

Where to Start

If you are unaware what digital surveillance is, a good place to start is learning about the different technologies that are used. Surveillance is not just security cameras. It can also include facial recognition, phone tracking, and automated license plate readers.

Questions to Consider

  • What types of surveillance technology are used by law enforcement?
  • How is personal information collected and stored?
  • Who has access to this information?

Resources Worth Exploring

The Electronic Frontier Foundation is a useful resource because it explains different types of surveillance.

The Atlas of Surveillance is another helpful tool. It allows readers to explore surveillance technologies used by law enforcement.

Surveillance Cameras

Surveillance cameras are one of the most common forms of digital surveillance. They are used in many public and private places. These cameras can record people's movements and sometimes use software to identify or track individuals.

Why Are They Used?

  • Helping prevent crime and theft.
  • Monitoring public spaces.
  • Providing video evidence during investigations.

What Are the Concerns?

Although cameras can help with safety, they also raise questions about privacy. People may not always know when they are being recorded or who can watch the footage. There are also concerns about how long recordings and if they are being shared.

Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs) are cameras that photograph vehicle license plates and use software to read the numbers and letters. Law enforcement agencies use these systems to help locate vehicles connected to investigations.

How Do They Work?

  • Cameras capture images of passing vehicles.
  • The information is stored in a database.
  • Police can search the information during investigations.

Privacy Concerns

License plate readers can help police find stolen vehicles and investigate crimes, but they also collect information on people who may not be commiting crimes.

The Electronic Frontier Foundation's information about Automated License Plate Readers is worth exploring because it explains how this technology works and why privacy advocates are concerned about its use.

Privacy, Law Enforcement, and Public Safety

Digital surveillance creates a balance between public safety and personal privacy. Law enforcement agencies use surveillance technology to help investigate crimes. However collecting and storing large amounts of data can be dangerous for the individuals.

Finding a Balance

Some important questions to consider include:

  • How can people know when their information is being collected?
  • Who should be responsible for protecting stored information?
  • What rules should exist to prevent misuse?

Why Accountability Matters

Surveillance technology should have clear rules about how information is collected, stored, and shared. People should also have ways to understand how these systems are being used.

The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) is worth exploring because it discusses surveillance technologies, civil liberties, and the importance of protecting people's privacy.
